Designing for Feeling with Desktop Beomgyu

Interaction Concept
I am developing Desktop Beomgyu, a small companion that should feel playful and comforting. The website combines three inputs: action radio buttons, a Movement speed slider from 50% to 180%, and a Quiet comfort checkbox. Selecting Run at a high speed creates an energetic response. Turning on Quiet comfort slows that chosen pace further. The checkbox also slows Hug and reduces the celebration bounce; the speed slider applies only to walking and running.

What It Represents
I want users to choose the kind of company they want. Movement, timing, and reassuring words communicate energy or calm, rather than relying only on color. The system responds to the combination of choices rather than treating every input as a separate effect.

UI Decisions
Radio buttons allow one action at a time. The slider lets visitors adjust pace continuously, while its percentage provides immediate feedback. Quiet comfort is a checkbox because it can combine with any action. Reset to Stay restores the initial action, size, comfort setting, and 100% speed. After feedback about readability, I enlarged labels, numbered the controls, and added a response card. I also reduced empty space and kept the preview visible alongside the controls while scrolling on desktop.

AI Support and Experimentation
I used AI to help write HTML, CSS, and JavaScript and to debug the interactions. I supplied the concept, media, and feedback. Early animations had disconnected joints, incorrect arm layers, and same-side arm and leg movement. I challenged those results and requested connected joints, specific arm layering, and opposite arm-leg timing. These revisions showed me that technically working animation can still communicate an unnatural movement.

Still Figuring Out
How much should movement change before users recognize a different feeling? I would like classmates to compare Run at 150% with Quiet comfort off and on, then tell me whether the difference feels energetic or calming. This is still a prototype, and I am continuing to refine the photo animation.
